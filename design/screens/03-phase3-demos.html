Three manufacturers have committed to dated solid-state EV launches, and only one of those dates has survived a full year without slipping.

Announced timelines

Toyota is targeting a 2027–28 limited launch on a sulfide-electrolyte cell co-developed with Idemitsu[1]. Samsung SDI’s pilot line is shipping samples but has published no volume date[5]. QuantumScape remains supplier-only — its QS-5 B-samples are in automotive testing at an independently reported 844 Wh/L[2].

What’s actually blocking volume

The gap is a yield problem, not a chemistry one. Separator defect rates compound across the stack: at 100 layers, a per-layer yield of 99.5% gives a cell yield near 61%[3].

MetricLi-ion (2026)Solid-state (projected)
Cell cost / kWh$78$180–240
Energy density520 Wh/L844 Wh/L
Volume productionnow2027–30

BNEF’s 2026 index puts pack-level parity no earlier than 2031 under its central case[4].

Bottom line: the chemistry works at sample scale; the economics do not work at fleet scale before the end of the decade.
